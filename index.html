Case studies

Five projects, in detail

Ridecell first, then Prezent AI. Each one includes what I would change.

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell | Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge

There was no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in spreadsheets, and employees had no tracked channel for questions.

What I did

Built a 40+ procedure SOP library with named owners and backups, launched ASKPeople on Jira and Confluence with tiered SLAs, and made Sapling the HR single source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected through Zapier.

Result

Growth from 3 countries to 5, 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, approval cycles cut from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and 5-10 tickets a day resolved in 24-48 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process, which created audit risk. Early on, some people felt a form would be too process-heavy.

What I did

Built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form that requires documentation and approval before any HRIS change, plus Notion New Hire and Offboarding workflows.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed before, and preboarding within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

  • Notion

  • Employee Action Form

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

A reorganization affected 18 employees across the US and India, and leaders had no way to see the cost of each scenario.

What I did

Built AI-enabled severance and cost modeling with Claude and Gemini that calculated severance, COBRA cost, and COBRA months owed and drafted the agreements, tracked on a shared spreadsheet for Payroll and IT.

Result

First-time cost visibility for a reorganization, and agreement prep cut from 20 minutes per employee to about 3 hours total.

What I would do differently

I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

  • Claude
  • Gemini

  • Severance modeling

How I would start

A proposed plan built around the responsibilities in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet the People team and the partners in IT, Legal, Finance, and Workplace to learn how work flows from signed offer to offboarding today.
  • Review Rippling data, permissions, and documentation, and map the lifecycle to find manual handoffs.
  • Learn how benefits, leave, workers' compensation, immigration cases, and contractor processes run today.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Run a focused People Operations review, like the 60-day audit I ran at Prezent AI, to rank gaps by risk and effort.
  • Draft a roadmap with owners, milestones, and measures of success.
  • Pick the first two or three workflows to redesign, and start the playbooks and quality checks.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Launch the first automations, for example an approval form for HRIS changes or preboarding that starts at offer acceptance.
  • Measure manual steps removed and accuracy gained, and adjust.
  • Set a coaching rhythm with regular feedback and quality review for the team.
By month 6

The outcome

  • The employee lifecycle runs on documented playbooks and operating standards.
  • Rippling data and documentation are audit-ready.
  • Repetitive work is automated, the team owns clear programs, and the roadmap is reviewed as the business changes.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with the People team and partners.
